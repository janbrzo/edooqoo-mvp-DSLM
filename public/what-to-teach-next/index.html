    What Should I Teach Next?

    Choose the next lesson by connecting one real learner goal with the strongest current evidence, then make a clear Continue, Repair, or Advance decision.

    By Jan BrzostowskiReviewed by Martha, ESL Methodology Reviewer, 10 years of experienceUpdated June 15, 2026
    Decide the next lessonBrowse worked examples

    Student context

    Name the adult learner’s real-world situation, current priority, CEFR context, and practical constraints.

    Evidence

    Review the strongest recent signal from live performance, homework, retrieval, or an upcoming communication event.

    Continue, repair, or advance

    Continue partial learning, repair a blocking prerequisite, or advance after independent transfer.

    Bounded lesson objective

    Define one observable performance, the minimum supporting language, and the evidence to collect next.

    The decision rule

    Repair when a missing prerequisite or recurring error blocks the goal. Continue when the learner has partial control, needs prompts, or the evidence is not strong enough. Advance only after accurate independent use without an established recurring target error.
