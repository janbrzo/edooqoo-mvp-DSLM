Advocating for ELL Students in Your School

March 2026 · Communication

ESL teachers are often the primary advocates for English Language Learners within their schools. Effective advocacy means using data, building relationships with mainstream teachers, and ensuring ELL students receive appropriate accommodations and support.

Key Advocacy Strategies

  1. Know the policies — Understand local and national ELL regulations
  2. Collect data — Track progress with standardized measures (CEFR, test scores)
  3. Educate colleagues — Help mainstream teachers understand ELL needs
  4. Request accommodations — Extended time, bilingual dictionaries, modified assessments
  5. Involve families — Keep parents informed and engaged

Common Accommodations for ELLs

  • Extended time on tests and assignments
  • Bilingual dictionaries during exams
  • Simplified instructions (not simplified content)
  • Visual supports and graphic organizers
  • Small group testing environment

FAQ

How do I advocate without overstepping?

Use data, not emotion. Present student progress evidence and research-backed accommodation requests. Build relationships with administrators before making demands. Position yourself as a resource, not a critic.
