TEFL Certification — Complete Guide for New Teachers

March 2026 · Professional Dev

If you're considering teaching English as a foreign language, a TEFL certification is your entry ticket. But with hundreds of programs ranging from $50 to $3,000, how do you choose? This guide explains the different certifications, what employers look for, and how to launch your teaching career.

What to Look for in a TEFL Course

  • Minimum 120 hours: Most employers require at least 120-hour certification. Avoid anything shorter.
  • Teaching practice: The best courses include observed teaching practice with real students (6+ hours minimum).
  • Accreditation: Look for courses accredited by recognized bodies (ACCREDITAT, AQUEDUTO, or backed by Cambridge/Trinity).
  • Practical focus: Good courses cover lesson planning, classroom management, grammar teaching, and activity design.
  • Job placement support: Many courses offer job boards, CV help, and employer connections.

Career Paths After TEFL

Teaching Abroad

Popular destinations: South Korea, Japan, China, Spain, Czech Republic, Thailand, Vietnam. Many positions include housing, flights, and competitive salaries. CELTA holders get premium placements.

Online Teaching

Teach from anywhere via platforms or independently. Requires good internet, a quiet space, and digital literacy. AI tools like Edooqoo help online teachers create professional materials instantly.

Private Tutoring

Build a client base locally or online. Higher hourly rates than school positions. Requires entrepreneurial skills alongside teaching skills.

Language School Employment

Work at established schools (British Council, International House, local language schools). Structured environment with curriculum support and professional development.

Building Your Skills After Certification

  • Specialization: Business English, exam preparation, young learners, academic English
  • Technology: Learn to use AI tools, digital platforms, and online teaching techniques
  • Professional development: Attend conferences, join teaching associations, pursue advanced certifications (DELTA, MA TESOL)
  • Portfolio building: Create sample lesson plans, worksheets, and teaching demonstrations

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a TEFL certificate if I'm a native speaker?

Technically some schools hire native speakers without TEFL, but it's becoming rare. A TEFL certificate shows you can actually TEACH, not just speak English. It also helps you be a better teacher — untrained teachers often struggle with grammar explanations, lesson planning, and classroom management.

Can non-native speakers get TEFL certified?

Absolutely! Many excellent English teachers are non-native speakers. TEFL courses are open to anyone with strong English skills (typically B2+). Non-native speakers often have advantages: they've learned English themselves, they understand learner difficulties, and they can explain grammar from a learner's perspective.

Is CELTA worth the extra cost?

If you plan to make teaching a career, yes. CELTA is recognized worldwide, includes intensive teaching practice, and opens doors to better-paying positions. If you're testing whether teaching is for you, start with a quality online TEFL and upgrade to CELTA later if you decide to continue.
