AI Lesson Planning Workflow

Step 1: Define Learning Objectives

Start with what students need to learn, not what AI can generate. Identify the grammar point, vocabulary set, skill focus, and CEFR level. AI is a tool to achieve your objectives, not replace your pedagogical judgment.

Step 2: Generate Core Materials

Use AI to generate worksheets tailored to your objectives. With Edooqoo, describe the topic, select exercise types, set the CEFR level, and the AI creates a complete worksheet with answer keys in under 2 minutes.

Step 3: Review and Customize

Always review AI-generated content. Check for accuracy, cultural appropriateness, and alignment with your teaching style. Edit, rearrange, or add exercises as needed. AI gives you a strong starting point, not a finished product.

Step 4: Differentiate

Generate multiple versions at different levels for the same topic. A B1 student and a B2 student can work on "travel vocabulary" with exercises tailored to their proficiency. AI makes differentiation practical rather than aspirational.

Step 5: Assign and Track

Assign worksheets as homework through platforms with tracking. AI grading provides instant feedback to students and saves teachers hours of manual correction. Use progress data to inform future lesson planning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-reliance: AI generates content, but you provide the pedagogy, relationships, and motivation
  • No review: Always check AI output for errors, cultural issues, and appropriateness
  • Generic content: Customize AI output for your specific students' interests and needs
  • Ignoring data: Use AI analytics to identify patterns and adjust your teaching

Frequently Asked Questions

Will AI replace English teachers?

No. AI handles repetitive tasks (content creation, grading, scheduling) so teachers can focus on what humans do best: building relationships, providing nuanced feedback, motivating students, and adapting in real-time to learning needs.

How do I know if AI-generated content is accurate?

Always review AI output. Specialized tools like Edooqoo are trained specifically for English teaching and produce more reliable content than general AI. But no AI is perfect — your expertise as a teacher is the quality filter.

Is using AI in teaching "cheating"?

Not at all. AI is a productivity tool, like a photocopier or a textbook. The pedagogical decisions, the teaching, the motivation, and the human connection remain entirely yours. AI simply makes the administrative aspects more efficient.
