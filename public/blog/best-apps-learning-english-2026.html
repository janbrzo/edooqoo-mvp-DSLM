Apps for Self-Study Learners

For Vocabulary Building

Anki remains the gold standard for spaced repetition, but requires setup effort. Quizlet offers pre-made decks. Edooqoo generates smart flashcards directly from lesson worksheets, connecting vocabulary study to classroom content.

For Grammar Practice

Traditional apps like Duolingo and Babbel cover grammar through pattern practice. For targeted grammar work, Edooqoo's AI worksheet generator creates exercises on specific grammar points at precise CEFR levels — useful for students who need focused practice assigned by their teacher.

For Speaking and Pronunciation

ELSA Speak uses AI to analyze pronunciation at the phoneme level. Cambly connects learners with native speakers for conversation practice. For structured speaking practice, teacher-assigned discussion questions and role-play activities provide guided speaking opportunities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can apps replace a human teacher?

No. Apps excel at drill, practice, and self-study, but they cannot replace the nuanced feedback, motivation, cultural context, and personalized guidance that a skilled teacher provides. The best approach combines teacher-led lessons with app-supported independent study.

Which app is best for complete beginners?

Duolingo's gamified approach works well for absolute beginners who need motivation. Once students reach A2, a teacher-guided approach with structured worksheets and homework becomes more effective for continued progress.

How do I recommend apps to my students?

Match the app to the student's specific needs and learning style. Assign specific app-based activities as homework rather than just saying "use an app." Set clear expectations: "Use Anki for 10 minutes daily to review this week's vocabulary."
